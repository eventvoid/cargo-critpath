what splitting a crate could do
  A split cannot be modelled exactly, because which half each dependent would need is not knowable
  without performing it. Each figure below is a range over a sweep of the division fraction from
  0.25 to 0.75 under both dependent assumptions. At an even division those two assumptions give the
  same critical path, so what the range is really made of is the sweep over the division. Each half
  is charged 0ms of per-crate overhead, because nobody has measured what a real split costs and
  inventing a number would be worse than charging nothing and saying so.

noise floor
  Only one timing report was supplied, so the run-to-run spread is unknown.
  No saving above can be checked against it, and this tool will not invent a threshold. Pass
  --timings once per clean build to measure it. On four real projects the spread ran from 2.3% to
  11.8% of the critical path, which is wide enough to swallow a recommendation whole.

executors
  The knee is at 2 executors: from there on the critical path dominates.
  Beyond the knee, total work divided between the executors falls under the critical path, so more
  of them cannot shorten this build. The knee is decided by the critical path and by total work,
  neither of which depends on the executor count, so it is not affected by the extrapolation error
  below in the way an absolute time is. It does still move with ordinary run-to-run variation,
  because total work does: repeated clean builds of ripgrep put it at 6 and at 7. Treat it as a
  small range, not a threshold.

     2     1215ms       1310ms      1310ms   graph       knee, pessimistic

    16      152ms       1310ms      1310ms   graph       optimistic

  The per-unit weights come from one build at -j 4 and are not invariant to it. Measured on two real
  projects, total unit work fell 23% to 30% between ten executors and two. Rows above -j 4 are
  therefore optimistic and rows below it pessimistic, by an amount this tool cannot correct for.
  The simulated column is not monotone in the executor count. Adding an executor can lengthen a
  greedy schedule, so a row longer than the one above it is a property of list scheduling and not an
  error.
